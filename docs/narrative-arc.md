# Wayfarer — Narrative Arc & Key Players

## The Arc in Three Acts

### Act 1 — Debt and Discovery (Tamori)

The game begins in **Tamori**, a feudal Japanese-inflected farming plane. Sarro accidentally burns Liris's barn. He pledges to get her where she needs to go. They don't like each other much yet.

Tamori has a problem: a Veil-tear swallowed the eastern rice fields three weeks ago. Spirits are disturbed. The harvest won't come in. The village blames itself — some old transgression, a failure of faith. The companions stumble into this.

Solving it means finding the tear, understanding what spilled through, and pushing it back. They meet the **Veil Wardens** — a diminished old order who once kept the sacred tears in balance, now scattered and bitter, watching their life's purpose collapse. A Warden named **Idris** (working name) tells them: *this is new. This is different. Someone is doing this.*

They find the Mender's trail leading out of Tamori through an unstable gate. They follow.

*Tone moment:* Before they leave, Liris helps with the harvest anyway. Sarro watches, not understanding why. She doesn't explain.

---

### Act 2 — The Chase (The Reach / Old Kaveth / other planes)

Plane to plane, always arriving after. Each world has its own complete small story — local people with local lives — and somewhere in it, the wound the Menders (or Extractors) left.

The companions start to understand the pattern: the Menders believe the Veil will collapse completely unless actively repaired. Their interventions are desperate, not malicious. The Extractors are a separate problem — harvesting Veil energy for commercial purposes, quietly, in the Menders' wake or sometimes ahead of them.

By mid-Act 2, the companions have:
- Encountered at least one Mender up close (briefly, not yet a confrontation)
- Made the **Extractor deal** (or refused it — see Choices)
- Learned from a Mender defector what the Menders are actually trying to do
- Begun to suspect the Veil doesn't need saving — it needs to be left alone

*Tone moment (The Reach):* Wide open cattle land. Nothing is wrong right now. The wound won't manifest for a few more days. The companions don't know that yet. They just walk. Liris talks about where she's going. Sarro mostly listens.

*Tone moment (Old Kaveth):* The ruins are beautiful. Something happened here a long time ago that feels connected to the Veil's original wound. They spend a night there. Nobody attacks them.

---

### Act 3 — Convergence

The companions reach Liris's destination. Whatever she was after — she gets there. It is and isn't what she expected. She has a choice about whether to stay.

Then: the main Mender is located. This is not a boss fight. It's a long conversation with someone who is exhausted and convinced and wrong. The companions have to make them *see* — which means having learned enough, and having the right thing to say.

If they succeed, the Mender stops. So do the Extractors (leverage, or a deal, or circumstance). The Veil begins — slowly, quietly — to heal. Not fixed. Beginning.

**The ending is small.** Two people, somewhere they weren't before, watching something settle. Sarro still hasn't found what he lost. He's less frantic about it. That might be enough.

## Key Players

### Idris — Veil Warden (Tamori)
Old. Precise. Grieves the old order without dramatizing it. Knows more about the Veil's nature than anyone the companions will meet. Not an ally exactly — she has her own views on what should be done — but a source of truth. She might give them information in exchange for closing a specific tear that's been eating at her for years.

### The Mender Defector — *(name TBD)*
Left the faction after watching a village get swallowed. Still believes in the Menders' *goal*, just not their methods. Will trade knowledge for protection — they have enemies in both factions now. A potential guide through Act 2. Morally complicated: they participated in the harm before they left.

### The Extractor Fence — *(name TBD)*
Buys and sells Veil artifacts. Knows where things are. Charming and practical and not ashamed of any of it. Not a villain — just someone doing commerce in a collapsing world. Could be a recurring contact across planes if the companions are willing to deal.

### The Lead Mender — *(name TBD)*
The person the companions are ultimately following. Brilliant, scared, has been at this for years. Has watched too many tears open to believe the Veil will heal on its own. The confrontation with them is the emotional climax. They need to be shown, not defeated.

### The Gods (The New Pantheon) — *background presence*
Sarro can occasionally commune with a divine ancestor — brief, costly, cryptic. The gods have their own stake in Veil stability (they emerged from the last collapse; another one might unmake them). They are not helpful in a direct way. They offer fragments. They have opinions about what Sarro is doing with his life.

## Meaningful Choices

### 1. The Extractor Deal
The Extractor Fence knows where the Menders are headed next. They'll tell you — if you agree to look the other way at what the Extractors are doing in this plane. It's not violent. Just profitable. Just damaging.

*Take it:* you get ahead of the Menders for once. The extraction continues. You see its effects later.
*Refuse:* slower path. The fence respects it, oddly. Maybe helps you a different way eventually.

### 2. The Warden's Ritual
Idris (or another Warden) has an old method for stabilizing a tear — slow, painful, uses up something precious. The Menders have a faster method you've seen. Both stress the Veil. The Warden's method is wrong more gently.

*Let the Warden try:* it costs them something real. The tear stabilizes imperfectly. You learn something about old Veil theory.
*Stop them:* you have to argue that nothing should be done. This is hard. They've been doing this for fifty years.
*Do nothing:* the tear continues. People are affected. But the Veil doesn't get worse.

### 3. The Mender You Understand
You catch a specific Mender mid-intervention — young, grieving, doing this because a tear took someone they loved. You could stop them by force. You could try to redirect them. You could let them finish and deal with the aftermath.

*Force:* works. They hate you. The Menders become more hostile.
*Redirect:* takes time and costs something. Might not work. If it does, they become complicated allies.
*Let it finish:* the damage happens. But you learn something from watching the full intervention play out.

### 4. Sarro's Portal
A gate opens — briefly, unstably — that seems to lead toward whatever Sarro lost. Taking it would stress the Veil noticeably. Staying is the responsible choice.

*He goes:* Liris can't follow. She waits. He comes back changed — found something, or found nothing, or found that the question is different than he thought.
*He stays:* the gate closes. He doesn't talk about it. Something shifts between them.

### 5. Liris Stays
When Liris reaches her destination, she has the option to stay. She didn't promise Sarro anything beyond this point. Traveling on is her choice.

*She stays:* Sarro continues alone, briefly. The final act changes tone. She might still be involved in the ending.
*She comes back:* she decided the thing she was after isn't what she needs right now. Or what she found changed the calculus. Either way, it's her choice.

## Tone Moments (Slow, Earned)

- Tending crops in Tamori before anything goes wrong
- A long walk across The Reach with nothing attacking them
- Sitting in the ruins of Old Kaveth at night
- Liris teaching Sarro something mundane that he's somehow never learned
- Sarro winning a fight and Liris not being impressed
- Finding a Mender's camp after they've left — the care they took, the notes they left, the wrong they meant to right
- The gods' silence when Sarro asks the question he most needs answered
- Reaching Liris's destination and it just being a place
