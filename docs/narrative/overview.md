# Wayfarer — Narrative Overview

## What it's about

A half-divine drifter and an angry mortal woman are following the trail of someone who is quietly destroying the world while trying to save it.

The central thesis: **grief makes people dangerous**. Every faction causing harm in this game believes they are preventing a worse harm. They are wrong — not in their love for what they're protecting, but in their certainty that they must act. The Veil doesn't need saving. It needs to be left alone. Convincing people of this is harder than any fight.

Tone references: *Blade of the Immortal*, *Planescape: Torment*, *Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind*. Earned endings. Quiet moments that matter more than battles.

---

## The World

The **Veil** connects all planes of existence. For most of history, Veil-tears were rare and sacred. Now they are erupting everywhere, swallowing fields and people whole. Something upstream is wrong.

The Veil responds to **conviction** — emotional clarity, genuine belief. Those without it can't safely transit; they get torn apart crossing. Sarro is exempt: his nephilim blood means the Veil simply recognizes him. Liris must earn every crossing.

The Veil is not dead. It is a living wound trying to heal itself. Every intervention — however loving — tears it open again.

---

## The Characters

### Sarro
Half-mortal, half-divine (New Pantheon bloodline). He passes through the Veil effortlessly, which means he's been drifting for years without anywhere feeling like home. He lost a specific person — **his sister** — to a Veil-tear years ago, before the current crisis. He's been moving ever since, half-looking, mostly not arriving.

He is not unhappy. He is hollow. He fights well because he's had years to practice not caring about outcomes.

His arc: **he stops searching backward and finds he's been accumulating forward without noticing**. When Liris takes his hand at the end, he doesn't pull away. He didn't expect to find himself holding onto something.

### Liris
A farmer from Tamori. Warm, patient, occasionally furious. Sarro's collision with the Veil — while fleeing a tavern brawl he caused — cracked a portal open near her home. Her **mother** stepped too close. Gone.

She can't go back. You can't re-cross your origin threshold once you've left; the Veil doesn't allow it. Sarro owes her not just a barn. He owes her her whole life.

She knows exactly where she's going and why. Her certainty is the thing Sarro doesn't have. Watching her — her competence, her groundedness in small things — is quietly disorienting for him.

Her arc: **she finds her mother alive in a distant plane. Her mother can't come home either. After a long moment, Liris takes Sarro's hand. Forward is enough**. Not forgiveness. Just the decision that what she has is worth having.

### Cael *(the Lead Mender)*
A middle-aged scholar. Quiet, careful, exhausted. Lost his **daughter** to a Veil-tear early in the crisis — not dead, somewhere in the between-places, unreachable. Has spent years performing Veil-stitching, genuinely closing tears. What he hasn't watched long enough to see: the stitching leaves scar tissue that weakens the surrounding Veil. He is closing wounds and creating fragility. He knows something is wrong. He tells himself he just needs to refine the technique.

He is not the final boss. He is the final conversation. He doesn't need to be defeated — he needs to be shown. The parallel to Liris lands here: she also lost someone to a tear. She found her mother by following slowly, trusting the Veil to do what it does. Cael acted compulsively. She walked steadily. Same grief. Different answer.

What he needs to hear: *"The Veil doesn't need you to save it. It just needs you to let it go."* And then something that makes him believe it — evidence, not argument. Liris's mother found her way through. Sarro transits freely and the Veil doesn't break. The system works when it's trusted.

If he can believe that — his daughter might still find her way back, without his help.

## The Shape

Three acts, six planes, ~15 hours. Each plane has a distinct aesthetic, a complete small story about ordinary people with ordinary lives, and somewhere in it, the wound the antagonists left.

```
Act 1 — Debt        Tamori only         ~4h
Act 2 — The Chase   The Reach, Old Kaveth, Verath, The Between    ~7h
Act 3 — Arrival     Ashan, The Convergence    ~4h
```

The companions are always arriving after. They solve the immediate wound, find the trail, move on. They glimpse Cael occasionally — close enough to start to understand him, not close enough to stop him. Until the end.

---

## Act 1 — Debt (Tamori)

Sarro burns Liris's barn. His proximity to the Veil tears a portal open. Her mother is gone. He pledges to get her there safely. She accepts — practically, not warmly.

Tamori has its own problem: a tear swallowed the eastern rice fields three weeks ago. The village blames itself. **Idris**, an aging Veil Warden, has been watching it for weeks and knows this tear is different — someone made it.

The companions help close the tear, meet Idris, learn the basics of what's happening and who's behind it. On their way out, Idris tells them: the Menders were in Tamori two weeks before the tear opened. Their technique caused it.

*Tone*: A slow morning in Liris's house before anything goes wrong. Sarro drinking tea while the village elder talks at him for an hour. Liris helping with the harvest anyway.

**Ends**: They follow the Mender trail through an unstable gate into Act 2.

---

## Act 2 — The Chase (The Reach → Old Kaveth → Verath → The Between)

Plane to plane. Each has a complete small story. The companions start to understand the Menders' genuine belief — and encounter the Extractors for the first time, a mercantile faction quietly harvesting Veil energy in the Menders' wake.

Key beats:
- **The Reach**: The Extractor Fence offers a deal — intel on Cael's next location, in exchange for looking the other way at the operation here. Take it or don't.
- **Old Kaveth**: Ruins built around the Veil, not despite it. They find **Mira**, a Mender defector who left after watching a village get swallowed. She knows Cael. She's useful and not entirely trustworthy. Bring her along or not.
- **Verath**: First glimpse of Cael — at the docks, watching a tear over the water. He sees them. He doesn't run. He leaves. Sarro's portal opens here.
- **The Between**: A transit goes wrong. Inside the Veil, they see the truth: the Menders' repairs are visible as scar tissue, rigid where the Veil should be flexible. And in other places — untouched places — the Veil is quietly healing on its own. They don't need to argue the thesis anymore. They've seen it.

*Tone*: The walk across The Reach with nothing pressing. Finding a Mender camp at dusk — the notes are meticulous and sad. Old Kaveth at night, beautiful, nothing attacks them. The nephilim carving Sarro spends a long time looking at.

**Ends**: They know where Cael is going. They know why. They go to find Liris's mother first.

---

## Act 3 — Arrival (Ashan → The Convergence)

**Ashan**: Warm, settled, quiet. They find Liris's mother alive. The reunion is real and not cinematic. Then they look for the way home — and it isn't there. Her mother can't go back. Liris can't either. The Veil doesn't allow re-entry through an origin threshold.

There is a long moment. Liris takes Sarro's hand.

Then: Cael's largest intervention is about to complete. If it does, the scar tissue cascades across dozens of planes. They go.

**The Convergence**: The conversation with Cael. He's tired. He already suspects he's wrong. He can't stop because stopping means admitting years of work made things worse. The companions show him — with Mira as witness, with Liris's mother as evidence, with Sarro himself (a nephilim transiting freely, proof the Veil has its own logic) — that letting go is not surrender. That his daughter might find her way back if the Veil stops being forced.

Two resolutions: if Liris's conviction is high enough, the final phase becomes a dialogue — the fight stops, Cael sits down, the companions leave him there. If not, a harder version. Both paths reach the same ending.

The intervention stops. The tears don't close overnight. But they stop multiplying.

**The ending**: Cael sits down somewhere. They leave him there. The Veil starts to settle. Not fixed — beginning.

Then: a portal opens nearby. Not dramatic. Not urgent. Just there, the way portals are when you've been traveling long enough.

Sarro looks at it. Looks at Liris. Says something like: *"Shall we continue?"*

Three options — player's final choice:

- **Go together.** They walk through. Screen goes to the Veil. Credits. The journey didn't end; they chose to keep going.
- **Go alone.** She stays. He goes. The screen goes to the Veil. A different kind of ending — she's arrived, he's still moving, and that's honest too.
- **Don't go.** They watch the portal close. Stay. Credits. The world doesn't need them right now. That's also enough.

The line doesn't change. The portal doesn't explain itself. The choice is entirely theirs.

*She was pulled into this. Now she's choosing.*

| Choice | Where | Stakes |
|--------|-------|--------|
| The Extractor Deal | The Reach | Speed vs. watching harm continue; Fence's arc if you deal |
| The Warden's Ritual | Tamori | Let Idris sacrifice something, stop her, or do nothing |
| The Mender You Understand | Act 2 mid | Force / redirect / let the intervention complete |
| Sarro's Portal | Verath | He goes (finds something or nothing, comes back changed) or stays (gate closes, something shifts between them) |
| Cael's Resolution | The Convergence | Dialogue ending gated on conviction score; otherwise combat to conclusion |
| The Final Portal | After the Convergence | Go together / go alone / don't go — Sarro asks, the choice is theirs |

## Open Questions (resolve before scene-level work)

- **What does Sarro find if he goes through his portal?** He should find something — not his sister, but evidence she was there and moved on. Gives him a direction without an answer.
- **Does the Extractor Fence appear in the ending?** Leaning yes — they followed Cael's operation out of self-interest and end up as a strange witness. Adds levity and moral complexity to the resolution.
- **Mira (defector) full backstory** — what specifically did she participate in and why did she leave?
- **What does the New Pantheon want?** Sarro's divine ancestor appears in The Between. What fragment do they give him?
- **Does Cael ever find his daughter?** Left deliberately open. The question is whether he can stop needing to know.
