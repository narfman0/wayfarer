# Act 3 — Forward (Ashan / The Convergence)

## Emotional Core

You find what you were looking for. It's not what you expected. You can't go home. That's okay.

This is the quietest act. The stakes are higher than anywhere else, but the tone drops. Long silences earn more here than action sequences.

---

## Ashan — Scene by Scene

*The plane Liris has been moving toward. Warm, settled. A place where someone could live quietly and did.*

**There is no wound here.** The Menders haven't come to Ashan. The Extractors haven't found it. The tears here are small and old and nearly closed. This plane is what the Veil looks like when left alone.

The companions feel this immediately when they arrive. The air is different. Lighter.

### Scene 1: The Search

They have an address, roughly. A description. The name the person was known by before they went through — and maybe a new name, if they made a life here.

The search is short. Ashan is not a maze. The person isn't hiding. They've been here long enough to have neighbors who know them, a trade, a daily routine. The companions find them through ordinary means — asking someone, being pointed down the right street.

### Scene 2: The Reunion

*Real, quiet, not cinematic.*

The person sees Liris. There's a moment — long, complicated — before either of them moves. What happens next depends on how long they've been apart, how much has changed, whether they'd given up on this reunion.

Sarro stands back. He is very clearly a guest. He busies himself with something unnecessary.

The conversation between Liris and the person she lost is not summarized or cut away. The player hears it. It's halting and specific and about small things alongside large ones — *I didn't know if you had enough warm clothes. I kept the garden. I couldn't keep the garden.*

### Scene 3: The Path Home

At some point — maybe immediately, maybe after a meal, maybe the next morning — they look at the way back. And it isn't there.

Liris knows how this works now. She's crossed enough tears to understand. You can't re-cross your origin threshold. The Veil doesn't allow it. The person she came for can't go back. Neither can she.

They sit with this for a while.

*There's no dramatic response. This is a game about people who accept things slowly.*

The person she lost has built a life here. It's not the life they planned. It's a life. Ashan is not a bad place to be unable to leave.

**Liris's choice:**
The choice the player makes here isn't binary. It's about how Liris holds this:
- Does she grieve openly, or quietly, or not yet?
- Does she tell the person it's all right before she knows if that's true?
- Does she look at Sarro when she's deciding?

### Scene 4: The Hand

That evening. The three of them sitting somewhere in Ashan — a porch, a field, wherever suits the moment.

Liris reaches over and takes Sarro's hand.

Not a declaration. Not forgiveness. Just: *forward is enough.* She has who she came for. She has him. That's something she didn't have when she started. It turns out to be enough.

Sarro doesn't pull away. He's spent years looking for something lost. He didn't expect to find himself holding onto something instead.

*No dialogue here. The game trusts the moment.*

**Tone beats:**
- The most domestic sequence in the game: eating dinner, sleeping in a real bed
- The person Liris found doing their daily work — ordinary, present, alive
- Sarro helping with something practical and being quietly bad at it
- Liris laughing once, at something small

## The Convergence — Scene by Scene

*Wherever Cael has been building toward. His largest intervention. Years in the making.*

**The wound here:** Not a wound yet — a wound about to happen. A massive controlled Veil-stitching on a scale the companions haven't seen. If it completes, the scar tissue will cascade. Tears will multiply in the rigid gaps for decades.

### Scene 1: Arriving in Time

For once, they're not arriving after. The information from the Defector (and possibly the Fence, who has their own reasons to be here) got them ahead of it.

The plane itself is unremarkable — rural, slightly elevated, good sight lines. Cael chose it for Veil-geometry reasons, not aesthetics. His team is already set up. The intervention will take hours. They have time.

### Scene 2: Getting to Cael

The Menders aren't hostile — they're focused. Getting through them isn't about combat; it's about either convincing junior Menders to step aside or moving carefully through a working operation. The Defector is enormously useful here: they know every protocol, every procedure. Their presence is disorienting for the Menders who remember them.

*Optional combat:* a Mender who doesn't stop when asked. Not a boss fight — just a person in the way who won't be moved. Short, regrettable, the player keeps going.

The Extractor Fence is here, watching from a safe distance. They came because the intervention would destabilize their operations in three adjacent planes. They're not sure what they're going to do about it. They end up as accidental witness to everything that follows.

### Scene 3: Cael

He's at the center of the operation. He sees the companions coming and he doesn't stop them. He finishes what he's doing in this moment — a calibration, a note, something small — and then he turns around.

He's tired. He looks like someone who has been at something too long.

He already knows they're going to try to stop him. He's also not sure he's going to fight them on it. He's been not-sure for a while.

**The conversation:**

This takes as long as it takes. The player participates, but it's not a dialogue tree with a win condition. It's a conversation between three people about grief and intervention and what the Veil actually needs.

Key beats:

*Cael, early:* "I've closed forty-one tears in six years. The network was collapsing when I started. I have data."

*The companions:* They've seen the scar tissue from inside the Veil. The old tear in Kaveth that was healing on its own. The Veil in Ashan, undisturbed, nearly closed.

*Cael, middle:* "You don't know that it heals. You've seen two data points."

*The Defector, if present:* "I've seen forty-one. None of them closed. They stitched. There's a difference." *(Beat.)* "I stayed longer than you did to find out."

*Cael, later:* He doesn't immediately break. He sits with it. He asks questions. He's not arguing to win — he's checking. This is someone running a real test on his own beliefs.

*The thing that changes it:* Liris tells him about the person she found in Ashan. Someone who went through a tear six years ago. Alive. On the other side, in a quiet place. The Veil carried them through. It didn't need repair — it just needed time.

*Cael:* Long silence. *"How long were they on the other side?"* *(She tells him.)* Another silence. *"That's about when I started."*

He sits down. He doesn't say anything else for a while.

### Scene 4: The Intervention Stops

Cael calls it off. Quietly, practically — he gives the instruction and his team follows it because they trust him. They don't understand. They'll ask questions later. He doesn't explain yet.

The Extractor Fence, from their vantage point, watches the operation shut down and calculates what this means for their business. It's complicated. They leave without speaking to anyone.

The Defector stands near Cael. They don't say *I told you so.* They don't say anything. Cael notices this and it matters to him.

### Scene 5: The Ending

The companions leave Cael there. Not as victors. Just done.

The Veil doesn't heal overnight. The tears don't close in a week. But they stop multiplying. Somewhere, in a field in Tamori, the eastern water clears.

Sarro and Liris somewhere — Ashan, probably, or a plane they haven't named yet. Not doing anything in particular.

Forward.

---

## Act 3 Summary

**What changed:**
- Liris found who she lost. Can't go home. Accepts it.
- Sarro stopped looking outward. Started holding on.
- Cael stopped. Not defeated — convinced. That distinction matters.
- The Veil begins to heal.

**The emotional answer to Act 1's question:** You can't go back. You keep going. You find, along the way, that what you're moving toward matters more than what you left.

**Final image:** Two people. A quiet place. Something beginning.
