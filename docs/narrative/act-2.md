# Act 2 — The Chase (The Reach / Old Kaveth / Verath)

## Emotional Core

Always arriving after. The companions start to understand who is doing this — and why — and that makes stopping them more complicated. Liris's anger at Sarro softens into something more like an uneasy alliance. The world gets stranger and more beautiful. Sarro nearly goes through his portal. The defector joins them.

This is the longest act. It earns the ending by making the player care about the right things.

---

## The Reach — Scene by Scene

*Midwestern pastoral. Cattle, wide sky, wooden fences, grain silos. The air smells like grass and coming rain.*

**The wound:** Cattle are walking into tears on the western fields and not coming back. The tears are multiplying. Ranchers are armed and frightened and arguing about whether this is the regional government's fault or the church's fault or each other's fault.

### Scene 1: Arriving Wrong

They come out of a tear in the middle of a cattle pen. The cattle are not happy. Sarro handles this better than expected (he's been through worse arrivals). Liris handles it with the calm of someone who grew up around animals. First moment of accidental competence together — brief, unacknowledged.

### Scene 2: The Extractor Operation

The ranchers know something is wrong with the western fields but haven't been able to get close — the tears are expanding. The companions go. They find: an Extractor operation. Harvesting Veil energy from the cluster of tears, which is why the tears aren't closing. The Extractors have equipment, people, and a permit (regional government, apparently). They're not hiding.

The Extractor Fence runs a trading post adjacent to the operation. Personable, curious, clearly not ashamed. Buys artifacts that come through the tears — bits of other worlds, strange materials. *"Someone has to. Better me than someone worse."*

**The choice — the Deal:**

The Fence knows where the Mender (their main target) is headed next. They've been tracking them independently — the Menders' interventions disturb Extractor operations, bad for business. They'll share the information: agree to leave this operation alone for two more weeks. *"The harvest will be done. We'll be gone. The tears will close on their own after."*

- *Take the deal:* you get ahead of the Menders for the first time. The extraction continues. Cattle keep going through tears. You see the damage when you pass through The Reach again later.
- *Refuse:* slower path through the act. The Fence is unrattled — they expected this. They help you in a different way later because they've decided you're interesting.
- *Negotiate:* if the player has built enough understanding of the Veil, they can argue that the extraction is making the Mender problem worse, which costs the Fence more in the long run. The Fence thinks about this. Agrees, partially, reluctantly.

### Scene 3: The Long Walk

Following the Mender's trail out of The Reach means crossing open land. No shortcuts. An afternoon and a night.

This scene is intentionally slow. The companions walk. They talk, or don't. Liris tells Sarro about the person she lost — obliquely at first, then directly. He listens. He doesn't offer comfort or explanation. She doesn't ask for it.

At dusk they find a Mender camp — abandoned recently, within days. The fire ring is cold. The notes are meticulously organized, left behind or lost. Sarro reads them. The person doing this is careful. They believe in what they're doing. There's no malice in the notes. There's grief.

Liris reads over his shoulder. *"They think they're fixing it."* Long pause. *"So did I."*

## Old Kaveth — Scene by Scene

*Ancient ruined city. Enormous stone structures, overgrown. The civilization that built this understood the Veil.*

**The wound:** The Menders removed a Veil anchor stone from the ruins — a relic that was passively stabilizing a very old tear sealed deep in the city. The tear is waking up. Parts of the ruins are slowly becoming somewhere else.

### Scene 1: The Ruins at Dusk

They arrive at the edge of the city near evening. The player can explore freely. The ruins are beautiful. Nothing attacks them (yet). The architecture tells a story: Kaveth was built *around* Veil-tears, not despite them. Gates incorporated into buildings. Channels that seem designed to direct Veil-flow. These people didn't fear the tears. They understood them.

**Tone moment:** Sarro finds a carved relief of a nephilim figure — old, clearly representing someone like him, in a position of... service? Protection? He stands there a long time. Liris finds him eventually. She looks at the carving, looks at him, doesn't say anything. They camp nearby.

### Scene 2: The Defector

Deep in the ruins, hiding: the Mender Defector. They came here because they knew about the anchor stone — followed the same trail, too late. They're wanted by the Extractors (took something on the way out of the faction) and they're not sure the companions won't turn them in.

They have Mender documents. Records of every intervention. Evidence that the techniques aren't working — not that they make things worse, just that they don't improve. The Defector left because they stopped believing. That's all. Not a dramatic break; just quiet loss of faith.

**What they reveal:** The Mender leader (Cael) isn't a true believer in the technique. He's a person with a loss, using the work to stay busy and avoid the grief. He knows something is wrong. He tells himself refinement is the answer. He's been telling himself that for years.

**The choice — Trust:**
- *Bring the Defector along:* they're a liability (Extractors follow them) and an asset (they have everything). They're morally complex. They participated in the harm. They're sorry in the particular way of someone who needs to do something about it.
- *Use their information and leave them:* clean but cold. They understand. They don't stop helping — they give the companions what they have. They take their chances alone.

### Scene 3: The Waking Tear

The old tear at the city's heart is growing. Parts of Kaveth are phasing — flickering between this world and wherever the tear connects. The companions need to stabilize it (without an anchor stone) or at least slow it down enough to get the information they need and leave.

This is the act's main challenge: a sustained encounter, not a single fight. The companions are working against time in a space that keeps changing around them. The Defector helps, if they came along.

What the tear reveals when they're close to it: the Veil *does* repair itself. Slowly. The anchor stone didn't seal this tear — the tear was naturally closing over centuries. The stone just marked it. The Menders have been "repairing" things that were healing on their own.

## Verath — Scene by Scene

*Coastal trading city. Mediterranean tone. Salt, ships, a permanent haze from nearby tears visible over the harbor.*

**The wound:** Tears have disrupted shipping lanes. Two factions in the city blame each other. One of them is working with the Menders; the other has figured this out and is furious. The city is on edge.

### Scene 1: A City With Sides

The companions arrive by boat (or through a tear that opens near the docks — their entry is disorienting). The city's politics are immediately apparent. They'll have to navigate it to find the Mender trail here.

There's a faction choice: who to align with while they're in Verath? The Mender-adjacent faction is idealistic, disorganized, believes in what Cael is doing. The opposing faction is mercantile, self-interested, correct about the damage, wrong about the solution. Neither is wrong about everything.

### Scene 2: Glimpsing Cael

At the docks, watching a tear that opened over the water: Cael. He's there. He sees the companions too. He doesn't run or fight — he looks at them the way you look at something you knew was coming. He leaves before they can reach him. Not running. Just done here.

This is the first time the player sees the face of what they've been following. He's not what they expected. He's tired. He's a middle-aged man who hasn't slept enough.

### Scene 3: Sarro's Portal

Late in their time in Verath — after the political situation is resolved (or not) — a gate opens over the water. Unstable. Small. It has a feeling Sarro hasn't felt before: *right direction*. Whatever he's been looking for, this goes toward it.

Liris notices him notice it. She doesn't say what it is. She just waits.

He has maybe ten minutes before it closes.

**The choice — Sarro's Portal:**

- *He goes:* Liris can't follow (she'd need conviction she doesn't have yet for an untethered transit). She waits on the dock. He's gone for what feels like no time and a long time. He comes back changed — found something, or nothing, or a version of what he lost that's become something else. He doesn't explain what he saw. She doesn't ask. Something between them is different.
- *He stays:* The gate closes. He watches it go. He doesn't explain this either. But he looks at Liris like he's decided something. She doesn't ask about that either.

*Either choice is complete. The game doesn't tell you which was right.*

---

## Act 2 Ends

The companions have Cael's destination. They know what he's trying to do. They know why — the loss, the grief, the years of telling himself the technique just needs refinement. They know the Veil doesn't need saving.

Now they have to catch up before the largest intervention yet.

But first: Liris's destination is on the way. Or close enough.

**What changed:**
- The player understands the Menders and feels the conflict (they're not wrong to want to help; they're wrong about how)
- The Defector exists as a witness and guide
- Sarro's internal arc has moved — whatever happened at his portal
- The companions' dynamic has quietly shifted from debt-and-anger toward something harder to name
- Cael has a face now

**The emotional question going into Act 3:** *Can you stop someone who's doing this for the right reasons?*
